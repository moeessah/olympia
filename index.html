Designing
for people
who would rather just talk

Two prototypes. About ten minutes. Olympia Family keeps relatives connected without turning an older person’s conversations into a shared record. Introductions helps one Olympia user meet another: one suggestion, two private yeses, one hello. It only works once there are enough Olympia users to meet.

01 · THE GAP

The family’s view.

Someone can talk to Olympia every day and still have very little contact with another person.

Family may live far away. Friends move, slow down, or die. Retirement and bereavement take away the reasons to leave the house or pick up the phone.

When a conversation suggests someone the person might enjoy talking to, Olympia can offer the introduction once. On a social platform, the person has to find someone, make contact and arrange the conversation themselves.

PRECEDENT
Age UK, Call in Time

Weekly calls between older people and volunteers, matched through a sign-up process.

The difference

Call in Time creates a scheduled volunteer relationship. Introductions works between Olympia users, from what each has already chosen to share. No profile to fill in.

07 · PILOT: LEARN BEFORE YOU SCALE

30

pairs in one language and time zone

The first test is whether the service works as a relationship. Matching starts only when one language and time zone has enough active Olympia users, and Olympia’s real numbers set that threshold. Calls are recorded only if someone separately agrees.

  1. Council8 to 12 people
  2. GateEnough users
  3. Pilot30 pairs
  4. EvidenceBoth want another call
Co-design before build

Form a council of 8 to 12 older adults before the service is built. Use short interviews, role-play and prototype run-throughs, and show people the scripts and failure cases early.

Recruit for range: different living situations, digital confidence and hearing, and some people who are not lonely at all.

THE USEFUL TEST

The council should be able to change the service.

TOO MUCH“Why does Olympia need to know that?”Take that information out.
BAD QUESTION“I would find that question awkward.”Rewrite the question.
NO EASY EXIT“I would want an easier way to stop this.”Strengthen the stop rule.
Evidence

After the first call, did both people choose another?

Proposed criterion: 7 in 10 pairs choose another call, with the threshold set by the council before the pilot begins. Thirty pairs can reveal problems and patterns but cannot establish a reliable rate. Also measure whether people understood the match, felt accurately represented and felt able to stop.

08 · BEFORE YOU GO

Why I built both

Paolo, your post says you are building an interface that learns the person. What Olympia learns first is what someone loves talking about, and the best person to share that with is often another Olympia user. Introductions is that step.

I would ship the family app first, because families are a big part of how Olympia grows. Emma can check on her mum without being invasive, the care of an older adult is shared between relatives instead of falling to the favourite child, and they can see their mother is being social beyond the family without hearing what she said.

I built the family app to show Introductions in context, and as the other arm of what technology can do: keep someone company, and help them find company.

APPENDIX
Notes on the family app

The promise

This app is the family's screen. It tells them when something needs attention and leaves the day's conversations with Margaret. Every screen carries one promise: peace of mind with her privacy intact.

Three decisions that shaped it

  • Memory Book belongs to Margaret. The stories are in her own words and she decides what is kept, by voice. Family reads and listens. She stays the only author.
  • Alerts record who reached out. Each card says who acted and how. The app does not record who looked, because read receipts would turn a calm app into a scoreboard between siblings. Anything older than a week settles into History.
  • Olympia always says what she is doing. A wellness check moves from "Olympia is checking in" to "Margaret is fine" or "Couldn't reach her", and a failure always offers Try again and Call Margaret. Every state has a next step.
More decisions
  • The badge lights for urgent alone. A badge promises something deserves an interruption. Reserving it for urgent keeps that promise believable. Amber items wait inside the tab.
  • Call Margaret lives on Today. Inside an alert it would suggest calling is for emergencies. On Today it says a check-in is something families do on any day.
  • Introductions lives inside Today. A tab would frame it as a signal that rises and falls. Inside Today it says what happened and stops.
  • Family splits into two decisions. My notifications affect only me. Who has access affects Margaret and the whole family, so adding someone needs her approval.
  • Escalation is visible step by step. If Margaret doesn't answer, Olympia calls her care contact and the family sees each step. If that fails too, the next move is theirs: call the contact, call Margaret, or try again.
  • Today shows how she is doing. Last spoke with Olympia, a good time to call, something to ask about from her own Memory Book, and who last called. Each helps family connect. None says what she said.

What the research says

  • Most caregiver apps are care hubs: task lists, vitals, shared logs. In the caregiver-app reviews I read, the real problem is handoffs, where information gets lost between people. Olympia Family is small on purpose and answers one question well.
  • Older adults and their adult children describe privacy in similar terms but weigh monitoring differently. In a small US study of 18 mothers and 10 of their adult children, the children liked each monitoring technology more than their mothers did and underestimated how much say the mothers should have (Berridge and Wetle, The Gerontologist, 2020). That is why Margaret approves who has access.
  • Alerts that cry wolf lose the home, so severity has to be rare to be believed.
  • In week one I would sit with an older adult at home, watch how she responds when Olympia mentions a family check-in, and rework this section around what I see.

Visual system

Green marks anything tappable. Red and amber are reserved for alert severity, and handled states pair colour with a tick or a word. A studio owns your visual identity, so I kept the system to what the prototype needed.

What family sees

Family sees one number, and only if Margaret agrees: how many social calls she has had. She chooses that when she says yes to Introductions and can turn it off by telling Olympia.

“Would you like Emma and David to see how many calls you’ve had? Only the number, never what was said.”

What is said on a call belongs to the two people on it. The one exception is a short list of danger phrases, which Olympia listens for.

Still open

  • The 9 second cooldown and the confirm step are my best guesses, and real users will show where they belong.
  • Every resolution in the prototype (Margaret, the care contact) is a timer standing in for a real conversation or call.
  • Handled notes are family-only. Open question: should Margaret see them?
  • Should family ever suggest a Memory Book topic? Olympia would tell Margaret who asked and she could say no, but it still adds pressure to a book meant to be hers. I would leave it out until families ask.
